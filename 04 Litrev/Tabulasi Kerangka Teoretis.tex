\section*{Executive Summary}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Executive Summary}

\subsection*{Recommended specification}

\begin{equation*}
\log Y_{it} = a + b_1 \log \mathrm{PMTB}_{it} + b_2 \log L_{it}
+ X_{it}'\gamma + u_i + \lambda_t + e_{it}
\end{equation*}

Two-way fixed effects, no scale restriction imposed, Driscoll--Kraay standard errors reported
alongside $t(33)$ critical values and a wild cluster bootstrap. Keep the conditioning set
$X$ sparse; see Section 2.

\subsection*{Minimum bias is a bracket, not a single number}

The two largest biases pull in opposite directions, so no single estimator minimises both.
Unobserved provincial heterogeneity biases the capital elasticity \emph{upward} and is removed
by fixed effects; measurement error in capital biases it \emph{downward} and is
\emph{amplified} by the same transformation (Griliches \& Hausman 1986). The proven response is
not to pick a winner but to report the interval the two ends define:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{4.4cm}p{4.6cm}p{5.8cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Estimator} & \textbf{Role} & \textbf{Dominant remaining bias} \\
\midrule
Pooled OLS & informal ceiling & unobserved provincial heterogeneity \\
Province fixed effects & intermediate & partial attenuation \\
\textbf{Two-way fixed effects} & \textbf{base specification} & attenuation, at its strongest \\
Long difference 2017 $\to$ 2024 & \textbf{upper end of the bracket} & least attenuated within-type estimate \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The long difference is the operative addition. True capital changes substantially over seven
years while measurement error does not accumulate at the same rate, so the signal-to-noise ratio
is at its highest and attenuation at its weakest --- the standard errors-in-variables remedy in
panels, and static throughout. Report $b_1$ as the interval running from the two-way estimate to
the long-difference estimate, and state which end each conclusion depends on.

\subsection*{How the two questions are answered}

\begin{center}

\textbf{Question} & \textbf{What the framework delivers} \\
\midrule

\subsection*{Three findings that shape the design}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=0.5em]

\item \textbf{TFP cannot be regressed on capital in a second stage.}
The two-way within transformation sweeps out $u_i$ along with the constant, leaving $e_{it}$
orthogonal to $\log \mathrm{PMTB}$ by the first-stage normal equations. The coefficient is
exactly zero --- an identity, not a finding. The link from investment to TFP is therefore a
\emph{decomposition} statement, not a regression result, and must be worded that way.

\item \textbf{The per-worker form imposes an assumption the data reject.}
Writing $\log(Y/L)$ on $\log(K/L)$ requires constant returns to scale; it does not merely
simplify. The Wald test rejects that restriction in every specification, so the unrestricted
form is the base and the per-worker form is reported as a diagnostic.

\item \textbf{Capital is a flow, and no reference defends that choice.}
The elasticity $b_1$ is biased downward, which means the TFP residual absorbs part of capital's
unmeasured contribution and is therefore overstated rather than understated. Direction is known;
magnitude is not, and the published firm-level bias figures cannot be borrowed.

\end{enumerate}

\section{The recommended specification}

\begin{equation*}
\log Y_{it} = a + b_1 \log \mathrm{PMTB}_{it} + b_2 \log L_{it}
+ X_{it}'\gamma + u_i + \lambda_t + e_{it}
\end{equation*}

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{3.3cm}p{5.8cm}p{6.6cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Term} & \textbf{Role} & \textbf{Supporting reference} \\
\midrule
$\log \mathrm{PMTB}$ & capital, no scale restriction imposed & Islam 1995; bias caveat: Collard-Wexler \& De Loecker 2016 \\
$\log L$ & labour, elasticity estimated rather than imposed & Islam 1995 \\
$X_{it}$ & conditioning set; see below & Holtz-Eakin 1994; Frankel \& Romer 1999 \\
$u_i$ & provincial productivity level, not a nuisance & Islam 1995; Di Liberto et al.\ 2008 \\
$\lambda_t$ & common productivity change over time & Muinelo-Gallo \& Roca-Sagal\'es 2011 \\
No second stage & avoids both orthogonality and the generated regressand & Wang \& Schmidt 2002; Pagan 1984 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection*{Reading the two effects}

The effect on \textbf{labour productivity} is $b_1$, the output elasticity of capital. Its
contribution to observed productivity growth is $b_1 \times g(K/L)$, the capital-deepening term
of the decomposition $g(Y) = b_1 g(K) + b_2 g(L) + \text{residual}$. Report period averages
only: the step from an investment flow to a capital stock requires a stable
investment-to-stock ratio, and that assumption breaks badly in 2020--21.

The effect on \textbf{TFP} is the residual of that same decomposition, together with the year
effects $\lambda_t$ as its time path. It is arithmetic on the estimated elasticities, not a
regression, and the wording must reflect that. Two warnings travel with it: the level of the
year effects is arbitrary and only changes carry meaning; and the year effects absorb every
aggregate shock, including the business cycle and terms of trade, not technology alone.

\subsection*{Controls: keep the set sparse}

A long control list looks careful and is usually the opposite here. The province effects already
absorb every time-invariant characteristic, and the year effects absorb every national shock, so
a control earns its place only if it varies within provinces over eight years \emph{and} is not
itself produced by the investment being measured. Most candidates fail one of the two.

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{4.6cm}p{4.0cm}p{6.2cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Candidate} & \textbf{Verdict} & \textbf{Reason} \\
\midrule
Human capital & include & varies within provinces; supported by Gardiner et al.\ 2020 \\
Openness, $(X{+}M)/\text{GRDP}$ & include & varies year to year; report as association only
(Frankel \& Romer 1999) \\
Electricity access, road length & \textbf{report both ways} & a large coefficient is a warning
sign, not a result: the effect vanished once regional fixed effects were imposed in
Holtz-Eakin (1994) \\
Sectoral employment shares & \textbf{exclude from the base} & likely a mediator; if $b_1$ drops
sharply when it enters, that confirms it \\
Internet, ports, airports & \textbf{exclude} & plausibly built \emph{by} the investment being
measured, so they sit between regressor and outcome (Cinelli et al.\ 2024) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The base specification is therefore the sparse one: capital, labour, both sets of fixed effects,
and at most two controls. Everything else belongs in a robustness column where the movement in
$b_1$ is itself reported.

% ==================== DEFENCE ====================
\section{Methodological defence, ready to paste}

\begin{quote}\small
The panel comprises 34 provinces observed over eight years, and a series of that length closes
off dynamic specifications: the bias of the within estimator in a model with a lagged dependent
variable is of order $1/T$ and therefore does not vanish as the number of provinces grows
(Nickell, 1981); it remains material even over thirty years (Judson and Owen, 1999). The
generalised method of moments usually invoked in its place is more fragile still in this
setting, because the instrument count grows quadratically in the length of the series while the
number of units is only 34, and instrument proliferation weakens the very Hansen test meant to
police validity (Roodman, 2009). First-order autocorrelation in the residuals is consequently
addressed on the inference side rather than by respecifying the model, since in regional panels
autocorrelation damages standard errors rather than point estimates (Bertrand et al., 2004);
Driscoll--Kraay standard errors absorb serial correlation and cross-provincial dependence
together (Driscoll and Kraay, 1998; Hoechle, 2007). That justification is asymptotic in the time
dimension, so at eight time points the reported $p$-values are upper bounds on confidence rather
than calibrated quantities (Vogelsang, 2012). Two-way fixed effects also fail the conditions for
causal identification when the previous year's output influences the current year's investment
(Imai and Kim, 2019), and every elasticity is therefore reported as a conditional association.
\end{quote}

% ==================== ROBUSTNESS ====================
\section{Robustness checks}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=0.25em]
\item Wild cluster bootstrap over provincial clusters (Cameron \& Miller 2015; Roodman et al.\ 2019).
\item Critical values from $t(33)$ rather than the standard normal (Vogelsang 2012).
\item Distributed lags of 2--3 years without a lagged dependent variable, reported as the sum of
coefficients (Beck \& Katz 2011; De Boef \& Keele 2008).
\item Leave-one-out provincial jackknife --- provincial economies differ greatly in size
(MacKinnon et al.\ 2023).
\item Three families of standard errors side by side, with the differences reported as they fall
(Hoechle 2007).
\item Province-specific linear trends, with the shift in $b_1$ reported as a finding rather than
hidden; then Oster bounds on the remaining selection (Besley \& Burgess 2004; Oster 2019).
\item The estimator ladder --- pooled, province fixed effects, two-way, long difference --- read
as an attenuation diagnostic rather than as four competing results (Griliches \& Hausman 1986).
\end{enumerate}

% ==================== LIMITATIONS ====================
\section{Limitations that must be published}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=0.4em]
\item \textbf{No reference defends using a GFCF flow as $K$ in a production function.}
Searched through Crossref, OpenAlex, and web search across six independent strands; nothing was
found. Olomola \& Osinubi (2018) do use GFCF as $K$ for Indonesia but never discuss the resulting
bias --- that is an omission, not a justification. If the report needs to state the conditions
under which the substitution is acceptable, that is our own reasoning and must be labelled as
such.

\item \textbf{The direction of the bias matters more than its size.} A downward-biased capital
elasticity means the residual absorbs part of capital's unmeasured contribution, so measured TFP
is overstated rather than understated. The published factor-of-two figure cannot be borrowed: it
measures a mismeasured stock in firm-level data, not a flow standing in for a stock in a
provincial panel.

\item \textbf{The direction of causality is contested in leading journals.} Blomstr\"om et al.\
(1996) find that growth precedes capital formation; Bond et al.\ (2010) find the opposite for
non-OECD economies. The tension is displayed, not resolved unilaterally.

\item \textbf{The decomposition holds on average, never year by year.} Treating GFCF growth as
capital-stock growth requires a stable investment-to-stock ratio, and 2020--21 violates it
outright.

\item \textbf{COVID subperiod splits have no methodological support.} Section 6.3 already uses
them. The options are to search further, or to report them as exploratory without inferential
claims.

\item \textbf{All 74 summaries are based on abstracts or metadata only.} No page-level quotations
exist yet. If the report needs direct quotation, the full texts must be opened first.
\end{enumerate}

% ==================== SCOPE ====================
\section{Scope note}

Realised FDI and domestic investment approvals are excluded from this framework by decision, so
the screening strands covering foreign presence --- horizontal and vertical spillovers,
absorptive capacity conditioned on foreign share, and crowding in versus crowding out --- do not
enter the specification. The seventeen references behind them remain in the library and can be
reactivated if the scope is revisited. Two results from that work still bind the present design
and are retained above: approvals data overlap PMTB by definition, which is why they cannot serve
as an instrument for it (D1--D2); and the coverage ratio of approvals to PMTB belongs in
Section 6.3 as a diagnostic rather than in a productivity equation.
